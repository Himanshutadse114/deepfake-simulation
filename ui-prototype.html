VOICE CLONING RISK

If this voice asked for money, would you trust it?

People naturally trust voices they recognise. A well-made cloned voice can create authority and urgency even when the sender is not who they claim to be.

What could an attacker try?

  • Impersonate a manager, colleague, supplier, friend or family member.
  • Ask for an urgent payment, OTP, password, recovery code or confidential file.
  • Use a familiar chat interface so the request feels routine and credible.
THE DEFENCEDo not verify a voice with more voice.

Use a known number, approved internal channel, or established verification process before acting.

This demo warns you. A real deepfake probably will not.

The same synthetic voice can be attached to a moving face. A convincing face and voice together can make fraudulent instructions feel authoritative.

Common pressure patterns

  • Urgent transfers or changes to payment details.
  • Requests for credentials, OTPs or confidential files.
  • Secrecy, urgency, unusual timing, or pressure to bypass normal approval.

PROFILE IMPERSONATION

One photo can be turned into a believable online identity.

In production, FLUX creates four synthetic images with different settings from the single consented portrait. Combined with a bio and follower count, that can manufacture familiarity.

How the trust trick works

  • Multiple believable photos make a fake account look established.
  • Copied names, bios and public information add context.
  • Once trust is established, the impersonator can move to requests for data, money or access.
REMEMBERDigital familiarity can be manufactured.

Verify unexpected accounts through a channel you already trust and report suspected impersonation.
